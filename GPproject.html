01 — Background

The company

Bestseller is a Danish fashion company behind global brands including Vero Moda, Jack & Jones, Only and Selected. Their growth is fuelled by performance marketing campaigns that send users directly to product pages — making page speed a revenue-critical metric.

The business problem
  • Performance marketing spend was not converting at expected rates.
  • Users arriving from paid ads were exiting immediately — before any meaningful interaction.
  • The hypothesis: the drop was a performance issue, not a targeting issue.

05 — Google PageSpeed Insights

Performance diagnostics

The product page was tested using Lighthouse under Slow 4G on an emulated Moto G Power. The results were severe.

A score of 38/100 placed the product page firmly in the "Poor" bracket. With the Largest Contentful Paint clocking in at 8 seconds, the page was effectively invisible to users by the time it rendered.

For mobile users arriving from paid ads — often on cellular connections — the experience was a blank screen that never resolved.

Root Causes
Heavy JavaScript execution
Main thread blocked for 9.1 seconds. No user interactions could be registered during this window. Users were staring at a locked, partially rendered page.
Excessive main-thread work
12.5 seconds of total main thread work — scripts, layout recalculations, and style processing consumed far beyond acceptable thresholds.
Large network payload
Over 7 MB transferred during initial load. On a Slow 4G connection, this alone adds several seconds of wait time before any rendering begins.
Critical request chains
Dependency chains between network requests introduced an additional 1.6 seconds of latency — resources were waiting on each other before loading.
06 — UX Engineering

Interface decisions driving the bloat

Beyond raw Lighthouse diagnostics, a closer inspection of the product page interface revealed specific design choices that were directly contributing to the performance problem.

Observation 01
Image navigation rendering

Every product page dynamically generated one DOM dot indicator per product image. A product with 20 images created 20 dots, 20 click handlers, and 20 layout recalculations — multiplied across hundreds of products viewed per session.

🧥
← →
Performance Impact

Excessive DOM nodes and JavaScript recalculation increase Total Blocking Time and delay rendering.

Recommended Fix

Replace dot navigation with simple in-frame arrow controls. Dramatic reduction in DOM complexity.

Observation 02
High-resolution product images

Fashion e-commerce demands high image quality — fabric texture, color accuracy and garment detail are all communicated photographically. As a result, images are uploaded at maximum resolution without format optimisation, accounting for over 2 MB of avoidable payload.

Business Constraint

Reducing resolution was not feasible — visual clarity is a primary purchasing signal in fashion.

Recommended Fix

Convert images to WebP format. Maintains visual quality while reducing file size by ~60%.

07 — Project Resolution

What was proposed and built

With the bottlenecks mapped, a two-pronged solution was designed: reduce the time-to-interactive for users arriving from paid ads, and expand the product discovery surface to catch non-converting visitors.

⚡
Lazy loading implementation

Images and non-critical assets were deferred until they entered the viewport. Users arriving from ads now saw rendered content significantly faster — critical for the first 3-second window where abandonment is highest.

🛍
Expanded product discovery

Users who landed but didn't convert were shown additional products inline. Instead of exiting, they were routed into browse mode — keeping them engaged in the Vero Moda ecosystem.
